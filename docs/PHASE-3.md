# Phase 3 — Fees, events, reports, hardening

Goal: the office runs the money and the calendar through the app and can answer the questions it gets asked. Fees sit on enrolments, payments are recorded and corrected in the open, families see their balance and how to pay; events reach the right families and collect registrations and consent; the reports page draws the demographic and attendance pictures; the audit log is readable; the three data-protection actions exist; the year can roll over. Then an accessibility and phone pass and the full E2E suite, and the app is ready for a first school.

Estimated ≈2 weeks. Scope and rationale: PLAN §17; tables in PLAN §5 "Fees", "Events & calendar" (not in the schema yet — each lands with its task as a migration).

Alongside the numbered tasks, this phase has carried the **area-by-area review passes** — admin 2–6, teacher 1–3, family 1–2, student 1, all under [`feedback/`](feedback/) — and the work that came out of them. That work is not listed as tasks below; the feedback documents are the record of what was asked for and what was done, and the decisions it produced are at the foot of this page.

Definition of done: on a seeded DB, admin records a €100 cash payment against a child, edits it to €120 and deletes another — each audited with before/after and the family's fees tab shows the right balance and the "How to pay" box; admin publishes a trip for Saturday classes with consent, the family registers and consents in one tap and the event page lists who is coming; the reports page shows the eight charts for the current year and exports CSV; the audit page filters by action and person; admin exports one family's data, deletes a declined application and anonymises a student who left; the rollover screen creates next year's enrolments from proposed classes. `pnpm test` covers balance derivation, event targeting, report counts and anonymisation; `pnpm test:e2e` runs record payment → family balance and consent to a trip; the accessibility pass leaves no axe violations on the main pages.

## Decisions made before Phase 3 (2026-09-16)

- No "overdue" concept: a fee is either outstanding or not. What matters is that an outstanding balance from a previous year is flagged to the family when they apply again and to the admin reviewing the application.
- Events with a fee show the amount; the money is collected outside the app.
- Reports break age down per year of age, not bands — a school's numbers are small enough.

## Decisions made during Phase 3

- 2026-09-16 (task 1) — A student's fee for a year is the fee on their current place; their payments are every payment against any of their places that year, so a class move never double-counts or loses money. A guardian's Payments tab lists the family's payments (all their children), with "Paid by" as a column, rather than only payments recorded against that guardian — the office asks "what has this family paid?", not "which parent handed over the cash?".

## Decisions made during Phase 3 (continued)

- 2026-09-17 (feedback 2) — A *family* is derived, not stored: the guardians who share a child (`lib/families.ts`). The office's Families page groups guardians that way and the fees "families still to pay" count uses the same grouping; there is no `families` table and no family id in the URL.
- 2026-09-17 (feedback 2) — Admin lists filter in the browser (`useUrlFilters`): the page loads the whole list, the URL still carries the filters, and CSV exports apply the same ones server-side. Only the academic year picker navigates.
- 2026-09-17 (feedback 2) — Moving a student into a full class is allowed, but only with an explicit tick; `moveStudent` refuses otherwise.
- 2026-09-23 (family feedback 1) — A notification may say **what it is about**: `notifications.subject_id` and `notifications.student_id` (migration 0008). Two columns, not a generic "entity" reference — those are the only two things a notification is ever about, and both are shown (subject badge, child's first name) rather than stored for later use.
- 2026-09-23 (family feedback 1) — A guardian sees the other parents on their children by **name and relationship only**, never their phone or email. In a separated family one parent's contact details are not the other's to hand out; the office has them.
- 2026-09-26 — A guardian's country of origin is asked **once, wherever they first meet it**: read-only in the registration wizard with a link to their account, unless they have never given one, in which case the wizard asks for it there and saves it to their account. Locking a blank field would have lost the answer for every new family, which was the point of asking.
- 2026-09-27 — The logo also becomes the **favicon** and the mark on every **email**. The tab and the title come from `generateMetadata` in the root layout, which reads settings; emails resolve it themselves in `lib/email/logo.ts` rather than making ten call sites remember to pass it, and fall back to no image if the origin can't be worked out after the response.
- 2026-09-26 — **The school's logo** is one optional image in Settings (`school_settings.logo_key`, migration 0011). It is served by `/api/logo`, the only route in the app that answers without a session, because the sign-in page needs it before anyone has one; that route can only ever serve the key held in settings, so nothing else in the bucket is reachable through it, and it sends a sandbox CSP so an SVG opened directly cannot run script. The upload itself goes through `/api/files` like every other file, and the action checks what R2 actually stored rather than trusting the browser.
- 2026-09-26 (student feedback 1) — **A family keeps their own child's record.** A guardian may change what they know better than the office — the child's name, date of birth, gender, school year and health notes — from the child's Details tab, audited as `student.update_by_guardian`. The student ID, the Arabic level and the class stay with the school, because they are the school's judgement or its filing, not the family's facts.
- 2026-09-26 (student feedback 1) — **A student sees their own record, read-only**, including their own allergies and medical notes and the names of whoever the school has down as looking after them. Not their carers' phone numbers or addresses: a child does not need them from the school, and the office holds them. Corrections go through a parent, and the page says so.
- 2026-09-23 (family feedback 2) — A family's class preference is a **level by name** (`students.preferred_class_name`, migration 0010), not a class row. A class is a level on a day; a parent may name the level without naming the day, and the old shape made the two inseparable. The office's inbox filters by level name, and a class's Applications tab lists whoever named that level on that day or on no day at all.
- 2026-09-23 (family feedback 2) — A guardian's country of origin is asked on their account, never per child: the wizard shows it disabled with a link to change it. A child taught at home still has a school year, so "Home schooled" is a fact beside the year rather than a replacement for it.
- 2026-09-29 (task 17) — **The school calendar is one table with a type**, not two systems. `events` already held the shape (schema v3); `lib/events.ts` splits the types into the **dates the school announces** — holiday, closure, exam, parent–teacher meeting, other — and the **activities a family may choose** — trip, camp, summer school, club, sports day, community. The 2026-09-23 note withdrawing "a holiday is just an event" was about the *words*, and it stands: the office adds "an entry", the two families are grouped wherever they are shown, and only an activity is ever something to join. `exam` was added to the type list (migration 0012, a table rebuild, since SQLite cannot alter a CHECK) because the office announces exam dates and everything else it puts on the calendar already had a type.
- 2026-09-29 (task 17, third pass) — **A class day is a calendar entry like any other**: a chip you can press for its hours, a row in the schedule, and for the office a way into the session. `LessonDay` therefore carries the session and the hours it runs, taken per viewer — staff see the whole day, a family the hours their child is there (`lib/timetable.ts`), which is the rule staff-only slots already followed.
- 2026-09-29 (task 17, third pass) — **Class days in the schedule are a switch, off by default, and then four weeks deep.** A year holds eighty class days and about eleven dates worth reading; listing all of both made a page eight screens long in which nothing could be found. Off by default because the schedule is read for the dates, and a family already knows it has class every Saturday; four weeks when it is on, because that is what anyone plans around. The heading says which is which rather than leaving the reader to guess why classes stop in November.
- 2026-09-29 (task 17, third pass) — **The calendar page has no second list.** The schedule view is the list, so "Add entry" sits beside the view switch, and the ways to add are the ways you would reach for: the button, a `+` on any day square, or a chip you already meant to change. Edit and Delete moved into the entry's popover, which means one way to act on an entry from either view.
- 2026-10-01 (task 11) — **The build reads no database.** The first real staging deploy failed in `next build`: `D1_ERROR: no such table: school_settings`, prerendering `/_not-found`. There is no `app/not-found.tsx`, so Next renders its own 404 through the root layout, whose `generateMetadata` asks for the school's name and logo — and a build has no request and no D1 binding, so `getCloudflareContext({ async: true })` hands it a local miniflare database, which on a fresh checkout has no tables. It worked on a laptop only because one was sitting in `.wrangler/state`, which also means that build baked the builder's own school name into the 404 page. `export const dynamic = "force-dynamic"` in the root layout says the true thing — nothing in this app is static, every page shows the school's name and logo — and takes the database out of the build. Static generation went from 60 pages to 8 and `/_not-found` became `ƒ`. Giving CI a database was the other option and the wrong one: it would have made the artifact depend on whatever rows the builder happened to have.
- 2026-10-01 (task 11) — **`typecheck` generates the route types it needs.** CI's first run failed on seven `Cannot find name 'LayoutProps'` errors that no laptop could reproduce. `LayoutProps` is generated, `next-env.d.ts` is gitignored, and `next.config.ts` points `distDir` at `.next-e2e` whenever `WRANGLER_STATE` is set — so after an e2e run the local `next-env.d.ts` imports types out of `.next-e2e`, and `pnpm check` passed on leftovers from a previous run. A clean checkout has neither directory, and the script deleted them anyway. `next typegen` now runs between the delete and `tsc`, which also rewrites `next-env.d.ts` back to `.next`, so an e2e run no longer leaves the next typecheck pointing somewhere else. The lesson is the general one about a check that is only ever run in a dirty tree: it was wrong from the day it was written and stayed quiet for three weeks because nothing ever ran it from scratch.
- 2026-10-01 (task 11) — **Staging deploys itself; production is released by hand.** One branch, not a `staging`/`prod` pair: a release branch has to be kept in sync with every release and its name carries nothing the commit SHA does not already carry, which is cost without a reader. So `main` passing CI deploys staging (`deploy-staging.yml`), and production is a `workflow_dispatch` on a ref you choose (`deploy-production.yml`), gated by a required reviewer on the GitHub environment. The two are **not** chained, which is the one real departure from the workflow this replaced: production has no domain and its `vars` are still placeholders, so a pipeline that reached it on a push would apply migrations to the real database and hand out cookies for an origin nobody owns. The production workflow fails on that placeholder before it migrates. The e2e suite stays *before* both deploys, against the local app — it writes registers, homework and payments, so pointing it at staging would scribble over the demo data and make the result depend on whatever state staging was left in; what runs after a deploy is a single `curl` of `/login`, because a Worker can upload cleanly and still 500 on boot from a missing secret. A `workflow_run` job also had to be pinned to `workflow_run.head_sha`, since it otherwise checks out the default branch rather than the commit CI tested.
- 2026-10-01 (task 11) — **Staging is deployed** at `albayan-staging.sweet-wave-5485.workers.dev`, on the free `workers.dev` address rather than a subdomain of `mai.ie`: staging is a throwaway, and a DNS record is a thing to maintain and a thing to leak. `BETTER_AUTH_URL` has to be the exact origin — cookies and every link in every email are built from it — so the Worker is deployed once to learn its hostname, the hostname is written into `wrangler.jsonc`, and it is deployed again. `BETTER_AUTH_SECRET` is generated per environment and never read by a human: `openssl rand -base64 32 | wrangler secret put` keeps it out of the shell history and off the disk. The staging database has no `school_settings` row and does not need one — `getSchoolSettings` falls back to defaults until the school is set up, which is what makes a fresh deploy's sign-in page render at all.
- 2026-09-30 (task 11) — **Brevo, not Resend, for transactional email.** Salah already has a Brevo account; the provider lives in one function in `lib/email/transport.ts`, so the swap was that function, its tests and four lines of documentation. Both are not kept: two providers mean two code paths and two sets of credentials for no gain, and swapping back is the same hour. `EMAIL_FROM` stays one string the way a mail client shows it — Brevo wants the name and the address apart, so `parseSender` splits it.
- 2026-09-30 (task 11) — **`bootstrap-admin --remote` promotes, it does not create.** Creating an account off the site means hashing a password the way Better Auth does, and a script that copies that by hand goes quietly wrong the day Better Auth changes it. So the first admin registers through the deployed site — the same path the school will use, which is worth exercising on a first deploy — and the script flips `is_admin`. It sets `email_verified` too, because on a first deploy the email provider usually is not configured yet and an admin who cannot receive the link cannot verify themselves. It talks to D1 through `wrangler d1 execute --remote`, which takes no bound parameters, so the one value it interpolates goes through a SQLite string escape.
- 2026-09-30 (task 11) — **No scheduled backup job.** D1's Time Travel already keeps 30 days and needs no setup. Beyond that, an export is every child's name, date of birth, address and medical notes, so it is run by a person onto storage the school controls, not copied nightly onto a CI artifact store or a third-party bucket where nobody is watching it. The README carries both commands.
- 2026-09-30 (task 11) — **The `images` binding is gone.** It was declared in `wrangler.jsonc` and used nowhere, and Wrangler warned that `env.staging` did not inherit it. Deleting it was the honest fix; duplicating an unused binding into a second environment is config that has not earned its place.
- 2026-09-29 (task 11) — **`pnpm typecheck` clears `.open-next` first.** `cloudflare-env.d.ts`, which Wrangler generates, types the self-reference binding as `typeof import("./.open-next/worker")`, so once a Worker build exists TypeScript follows that import into a 22 MB generated bundle and fails on its minified private fields. `exclude` cannot help: it filters what the `include` globs find, not what another file imports. The build is regenerated by `pnpm preview` and `pnpm deploy` before either uses it, and is gitignored, so deleting it costs a rebuild and nothing else — cheaper than loosening the binding's type, which `pnpm cf-typegen` would undo. Without this, every `pnpm check` after a deploy fails; CI escaped it only because it checks before it builds.
- 2026-09-29 (task 11) — **The fourteen migrations were collapsed into one.** Every schema change so far was made against a local database that nobody else had a copy of, and both remote databases were created empty that day (0 tables, 0 queries), so there was nothing to preserve and this was the last moment it was free: once production has rows, a squash is off the table forever. `drizzle/0000_schema.sql` is now generated from `lib/db/schema` alone, which also retired the two hand-written table rebuilds (0012, 0013) that existed only because SQLite cannot alter a CHECK constraint. Decision lines above still name the old migration numbers; they refer to work, not to files that exist. The squash was proved a no-op by diffing the schema of a database built from the fourteen against one built from the one, and by `pnpm db:generate` then reporting no changes.
- 2026-09-29 (task 11) — **The squash found a real drift.** `notifications.student_id` is declared in `lib/db/schema/platform.ts` with `onDelete: "cascade"`, but migration 0008 added the column with a bare `REFERENCES students(id)` and no delete rule, so every database built from the incremental migrations had `NO ACTION` instead. The generated schema matches the declaration, so the cascade is now real everywhere. It would have surfaced as a foreign-key error the first time task 8 deleted a student who had ever been notified.
- 2026-09-29 (task 17, fourth pass) — **The teacher gets the calendar back**, removed on 2026-09-19 when it had nothing on it but term days. It now has the school's dates, the activities aimed at the classes they take, their class days — and the **staff meetings**, which no family or student calendar can show. That is the one viewer who reads a staff-audience entry, so `ViewerScope` gained a `staff` flag that only `scopeForTeacher` sets; every other caller is unchanged and still cannot see one. **Calendar** sits above **My timetable** in the teacher's navigation: the calendar is the year, the timetable is the week that repeats inside it.
- 2026-09-29 (task 17, fourth pass) — **Siblings share a chip.** Both children of a family are usually in the same session on the same day, so labelling a class day per child put the same day and the same hours on the grid twice — and, since the chip's key is the session and the label, twice under one key. One entry per session now, naming whoever is in it ("Amira & Yusuf: Saturday"); `lib/db/queries/calendar.test.ts` holds the case that caught it.
- 2026-09-29 (task 17, second pass) — **Staff meetings are entries with an audience of their own.** `staff` joins whole school / some days / some classes (migration 0013, with a `staff_meeting` type), rather than a "visible to" flag beside it: audience already answers "who is this for", and a staff-only entry is then neither whole-school nor targeted, so the family and student query cannot return one by construction. A staff meeting picks that audience by itself when the office chooses the type.
- 2026-09-29 (task 17, second pass) — **A calendar entry wears its kind**: a colour and an icon per type (DESIGN §4.10), clay for the days the school is shut, saffron for exams, tile for parent–teacher meetings, grey for staff, plum for activities. Colour never carries it alone — every chip has its icon — and subject colours stay out of the calendar. Chips are buttons: pressing one opens a popover with what the school knows, and the office gets Edit in it, so the grid is as good a way in as the list.
- 2026-09-29 (task 17) — An entry's `startAt`/`endAt` hold **a date, or a date and a time**: `2026-10-24` for a week-long break, `2026-11-21T10:00` for meetings that start at ten.
